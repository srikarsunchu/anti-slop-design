# Baby sleep category — the anti-reference

Source: App Store listings for Sleeptot (id1229010143), Sound Sleeper
(id507967709), Huckleberry (id1169136078), White Noise Lite (id292987597),
Endel (id1346247457). Extracted 2026-09-04 from listing screenshots. This
file records what the category looks like so a new entrant can avoid reading
as one more of them. Values are read from screenshots and marked guessed.

## What every baby app does

- **Pastel ground and stock photography.** Sleeptot is blush pink with a
  pink hair dryer product shot and a sleeping baby photo. Sound Sleeper is
  blush plus mustard yellow with a mother-and-baby photo. The photos are
  stock; the product looks like a greeting card.
- **All-caps geometric sans headlines.** "THE MIRACLE SLEEP APP", "HELP YOUR
  BABY FALL ASLEEP IN MINUTES", "THE #1 BABY SLEEP APP". Superlatives in
  place of a claim.
- **Photo tiles for sounds.** A two-column grid of photos (a car seat, a fan,
  a waterfall) with a caption under each. Rounded corners, drop shadows.
- **Four-tab bar.** Sleep / Tracking / Relaxation / Info (Sound Sleeper),
  Sounds / Favourites / More (Sleeptot). The template bar.
- **Duplicate controls.** Sound Sleeper's player has Play, Pause, and Listen
  as three equal buttons; Sleeptot has Play and Stop as two pills plus a
  duration slider and a volume slider. Nothing is primary.
- **Badges as ground.** Laurel wreaths, "App of the Day", star rows.
- **Nothing is dim.** Every screen is designed for a bright room. None of them
  shows the phone lying on a nightstand.

## What the generic apps do

- **White Noise Lite**: starry sky ground, a grid of photo tiles, a "Mix Pad"
  of overlapping green circles, and one good screen: a black nightstand clock
  with 5:08 in huge thin digits and "Sound Alarm at 8:00 AM" above it.
- **Endel**: pure black, white line art, one glyph per soundscape, italic
  serif headings inside the app. Near-monochrome, no accent, and it reads
  premium because of it.
- **Dark Noise**: see `dark-noise.md`. Violet by default, list-first, the
  craft benchmark.

## What Huckleberry does (the tracker, not a sound app)

Colored full-width blocks (teal Sleep, coral Nursing, yellow Diaper, violet
Pumping), one tap to log, a lock-screen Live Activity ("SweetSpot near
11:00am"), a calendar heatmap of sleep. Utility done for tired parents in a
daytime palette. The Live Activity is the pattern to take; the palette is not.

## Color
- baby apps: blush #F4D6D9 region, mustard #F5B400 region, dark charcoal
  player cards; count exceeds five colors per screen
- generic apps: violet accent (#7C4DFF region) or none
- accent used for: everything, including hearts and dividers

## Type
Geometric sans (Quicksand, Montserrat, Poppins region) in 700 caps for
headlines; body at 15 to 17 regular. Guessed from letterforms.

## Icons
Mixed sets: outline clock next to filled heart next to a monoline ear. No
single grammar in the baby apps. Endel and Dark Noise each keep one.

## Hierarchy read
The listing screenshots put the headline over the device; the device is
tilted and cropped, so the actual UI is never the subject. Inside the app,
Play, Pause, and Stop compete. There is no one thing.

## The lesson, as a rule
"The category signals 'baby' with blush pink, stock photos, and caps
superlatives, so a new entrant that wants to read as designed must refuse all
three and instead show the one screen a mother actually sees at 3am, dim, with
one thing to tap."

Second: "Every incumbent makes Play, Pause, and Stop equal buttons, so make
one control that is the whole product and let everything else be a sheet."
