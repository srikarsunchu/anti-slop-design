# One idea, made physical

Every good page is built around one idea, and that idea is visible as a thing,
not stated as a tagline.

- A context-file product shows a file with a diff in a window. The metaphor is
  the anchor.
- A shot-list tool shows the shot list, with actual timings, mid-shoot.
- An origami-named product uses folded paper as its mark and faint line art as
  its ground.

A generated page states the benefit in a headline and then shows three cards.
The idea never becomes an object.

## How to find it

Answer in one sentence: "This design's idea is ___." Then answer: "The thing on
the page that IS that idea is ___." The second answer must be a component, an
image, a texture, or a demo. If it is a sentence, keep going.

## How it drives everything

- Layout: the anchor gets the most space and the most contrast.
- Imagery: the ground supports the idea (sky for openness, dark dither for a
  night shoot, paper for writing).
- Color: the accent, if any, is sampled from the idea's imagery.
- Icons: one set whose grammar matches the idea's tone.
- Copy: every section extends the idea instead of listing features.

## Rule

One idea per page. One subject per viewport. Whitespace is earned by a focal
point; whitespace around nothing is emptiness.
